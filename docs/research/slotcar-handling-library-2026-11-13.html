How to read this library

Stock assumed means no relevant change was disclosed, following the requested convention. It does not confirm the parts, traction magnet, or maintenance history. Explicit preparation, club modifications and magnet removal take precedence. Unknown fields remain unknown; we do not silently substitute modern catalogue specifications for older or modified cars.

AW = anglewinder; IL = inline; SW = sidewinder. Layouts marked “inferred” need confirmation. Tyre width is separate from wheel width and axle spacing. Nominal tyre dimensions are not measured contact-patch widths. Wheelbase is front-to-rear axle spacing; guide-to-rear-axle distance is recorded separately.

Reported mass belongs to the reviewed specimen and weighing stage. Reference specifications are labelled separately and must not be treated as modified race weights. “Non-magnet” describes traction-magnet use; it does not establish zero motor magnetic attraction.

Sources include forum discussions, club reports and firsthand reviews. Search covered English, French, German and Spanish material; this is a curated sample, not an exhaustive survey. The extension includes older releases because their owners document setup changes; those results must not be assigned to current revisions automatically. Inaccessible follow-up pages, unattributed recommendations, 1:24 cars, general brand praise, joke setups and marketing-only claims were excluded. Indexed text is identified where a direct page could not be opened.

What the nine added brands contribute

These are evidence-informed research directions for future advice, not new application rules. Reports often change several things together, so neither lap-time differences nor brand rankings establish a causal relationship.

  • Grip and stability must be considered together. The Elva test reports less sliding but more wheel lift after a tyre change, then improvement with float adjustment (S41).
  • Check actual front support. The rebuilt Ninco Mercedes improved after front-wheel height adjustment; its support pattern cannot be reduced to an assumed tripod (S33).
  • Distinguish bumps from cornering failures. Small front ballast helped the Bullitt over humps (S34); that is not proof it will cure every sliding or tipping car.
  • Rule out interference before blaming geometry. Capri body/chassis contact (S36) and Shadow guide drag (S31) show why clearance and free movement belong in the evidence record.
  • Record the tested generation and complete change. FlySlot inline and older Fly sidewinder cars differ beyond motor angle (S28, S29). The Ninco chassis conversion also changed suspension, gears and contact geometry (S33).
  • Keep disagreement. The two MR Mazda comparisons favour different cars under different preparation and track conditions (S30). Do not turn either into a universal model ranking.

More models broaden the range of possible explanations. Stronger numerical predictions still require repeated measurements with known track surface, magnet state, tyres, front contact, mass distribution and a recorded baseline. Most sources do not supply those together.

White kits, proxy follow-ups and tested changes

New records R61–R74. White kits are included as individual builds. A white assembled RTR, an unbuilt kit, a retail kit and a preproduction sample are different starting points. Search white kit, confirmed follow-up, no meaningful or custom conversion to explore this pass.

The strongest additions preserve the sequence: original setup, observed problem, actual change and subsequent result. Multiple stages of one car remain one evidence history. A proposed fix is never promoted to a successful repair simply because the discussion ends.

  • Tyre changes can help, but success is not automatic. Compare the confirmed Austin replacement history (S44, S45) with the Formula profile test that found no meaningful gain (S48).
  • Drivetrain conversions need a complete change record. The Skyline provides a useful staged build, not proof that SW inherently beats AW (S51).
  • Fastest and easiest are separate observations. The GT3 comparison records abrupt breakaway, two-wheel running and consistency alongside lap performance (S49).
  • Preserve failures in the evidence itself. Missing magnet status stays unknown; inconsistent lap totals are excluded rather than repaired by guesswork. Search results, owner interpretations and actual measurements remain distinguishable.

For a white-kit record: retain builder, kit/SKU and revision, chassis and motor pod, motor/gearing, fitted tyres, completed body mass, paint/interior/glazing changes, ballast placement, mounting freedom, test conditions and the actual outcome. Leave unavailable fields blank. Do not assume a white kit is lighter, straighter or faster than an RTR.

Focused follow-up: stronger evidence, not just more cars

New records R75–R79. This pass investigated linked proxy inspections, repeated tests of one finished car and decoration mass. The first two produced usable evidence; the third remains an unresolved measurement gap.

1. Proxy inspection + track feedback: found

The 2025 Global GT report can be joined to venue reports by entrant, car number and model. R77–R79 retain that identity. The inspection includes tyre widths, front/rear weight readings, clearance and motor-attraction readings. S54–S57.

Interpretation: evaluate the same entry in different corner types. A class victory does not mean every part of its handling was ideal. Repairs, driver assignments, lane differences and race format must stay attached to the observation.

2. Repeated finished-car tests: found

The Maserati diary follows preparation and later ballast/gearing experiments on one specimen. R75 and R76 belong to one history. It documents subjective stability as well as best laps, and explicitly identifies combined changes. S58, S59.

Interpretation: distinguish fastest isolated lap from the driver's preferred, manageable setup. Repeating a baseline is useful, but does not replace lap distributions or fully controlled comparisons.

3. Same-scale decoration mass: still a gap

No fully documented same-body, same-scale, unchanged-assembly pair was verified in this pass. The candidates below remain outside the handling sample and cannot supply a default paint penalty.

Needed: the same identified shell, the same fitted parts before/after, scale resolution, cured finish and separate records for added glazing, cockpit, glue and decoration.

Measurement discipline: proxy total masses here are sums of the published F/R readings, explicitly labelled derived. Without the support and weighing procedure these do not establish CG location. Motor attraction in gf is a force reading, not body mass; test-fixture details are still needed for comparison with another device. “Stock body” does not mean a stock drivetrain or chassis.

Decoration-weight leads — not calibration data

  • NSR Mosler, pasionslot76, 2010: indexed posts describe a 14.6 g body reference and 22 g decorated result, then roughly 8 g added. It is unclear whether the starting figure was measured on this specimen or taken from the product description. Same-scale use and unchanged assembly are unverified. Retain as a lead, not a paint-only measurement. S60.
  • Scratch-built 1:32 Alfa TCR, Marsu, 2022: reported completion adds 6.3 g, including lights, glazing and interior; the author assigns about 2 g to paint/varnish. Useful itemisation, but not an independently verified paint-only pair. This custom resin body is outside the requested manufacturer handling sample. S61.
  • NSR Formula: the earlier painted/reference comparison uses different bodies and whole-gram readings. It also cannot isolate decoration mass. S48.

What this can teach Telemetry

Separate the symptom from its cause

Record entry, mid-corner, exit, braking and bumps separately. Rear sliding, hopping, front deslotting and tipping should not collapse into one “bad handling” label. Predictable drift can be desirable; ask what the driver wants to change.

Compare within the same conditions

Start with same car, driver, lane, surface, controller and voltage. Record tyre preparation, traction magnet and measured motor attraction. A layout swap also changes other components unless those are deliberately controlled.

Preserve unsuccessful changes

Keep improved, worsened, unchanged and untested outcomes. Distinguish an owner's explanation from a demonstrated cause. Several simultaneous changes support the complete setup, not each individual part.

Ask only the next useful question

With only “my car slides”, offer a short troubleshooting route. With a matched model and track, show relevant owner experience. With measurements or a before/after test, explain more specific options and what to check next.

Suggested structure for future records

Keep car identity, setup, observation and change/test separate. Each observation should retain a source, date, owner/test group and evidence status. Same-author cross-posts and different liveries of the same platform must not inflate support.

  • Car: brand, model, SKU, scale, chassis generation, motor layout and build origin (RTR, assembled white RTR, white kit, prototype or custom conversion).
  • Setup: motor and gearing; tyre compound, actual fitted diameter/width and preparation; total/body/ballast mass and ballast location; wheelbase, guide span and rear contact spacing; chassis/pod/body movement.
  • Conditions: surface, rail material, traction magnet, motor magnetic-force measurement, voltage, controller/braking, lane and bumps.
  • Behaviour: symptom, corner phase, direction, controllability, repeatability and driver preference.
  • Test: before/after configuration, changed variables, lap consistency, deslot count, observed handling and whether the change was reversed to check repeatability.

Every field can remain optional. The system should use what is known, clearly identify missing information and avoid inventing grip coefficients, centre-of-gravity height or performance scores from a model name.

What is still missing for numerical correlations

This collection does not establish a numerical relationship between ballast, tyre width, wheelbase and drift/tipping risk. Most reports omit fitted measurements; selection bias and driver/track differences remain substantial. The wider brand sample now helps identify different failure modes. The next step toward numerical correlations is repeated, documented testing of the same platforms under comparable conditions. Reference dimensions should be verified against the exact SKU before enriching a record.

A suitable first study would hold tyres, track and power constant, record baseline laps/deslots, change one setting, repeat, then return to baseline. Compare handling and consistency as well as fastest lap. Motor-layout studies need to record the resulting motor, gearing, mass distribution and magnetic-force changes; layout alone cannot receive credit.

Where further research would be useful

The focused follow-up has now found linked proxy records and a useful repeated-car diary. Next priorities are full lap/deslot records for those same entries, confirmed unchanged setups between venues, documented weighing fixtures, and genuine before/after decoration measurements. More models alone would not resolve these gaps. Photo-only builds and unresolved advice threads can supply leads, but not confirmed handling improvements.
